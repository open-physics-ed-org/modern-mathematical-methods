28 Sep 23 - Activity: Superposition of Fields

Coulomb’s Law We’ve seen that the electric field of a set of point charges if given by the vector sum of the contributions from each charge: $$\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_i \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{q_i}{(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}’_i)^3} (\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}’_i)$$ where $\mathbf{r}’_i$ is the location of the charge relative to the origin, and $\mathbf{r}$ is the location of the field point relative to the origin. This net electric field is the result of the superposition of the electric fields from each charge. But here the charges are discrete, we can identify each charge uniquely in terms of it’s charge and location. This concept can be abstracted to a continuous distribution of charge, where we can identify the charge at any point in space by the charge density $\rho(\mathbf{r})$.
